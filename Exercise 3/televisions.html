Does a Bigger TV Really Cost More to Run?

Screen size is usually the first thing people compare when TV shopping — but the sticker price is only half the story. Every registered TV sold in Australia carries a labelled energy consumption figure (kWh per year), based on standard test conditions. We used the Australian Government's Energy Rating registration database — 4,724 currently approved TV models — to see how much screen size actually affects what you'll pay to run it.

1. Bigger screens cost a lot more to run — and it's not a small effect

We grouped every model in the database into the screen-size bands shoppers usually compare in-store, and looked at the average labelled energy use for each. Screen size and energy consumption are very strongly related (r = 0.86) — this isn't noise, it's a consistent pattern across thousands of models.

Why this matters

A jump from a 32" to an 80"+ TV isn't just a bigger upfront purchase — it's roughly an extra $230 a year in running costs at typical use, every year you own it. Over a 7–8 year TV lifespan, that's close to $1,800 — often more than the price gap between the two TVs themselves.

2. If you want a huge screen, the technology you choose matters more than you'd think

OLED TVs have a reputation for being power-hungry compared to standard LCD/LED screens. Our data tells a different story once you focus specifically on the size band where OLED is most commonly sold — 80" and above.

Average labelled annual energy consumption and estimated running cost for 80"+ TVs, split by panel technology. LCD (non-LED backlit) has a small sample in this band (n = 18) and should be read cautiously; LCD (LED) (n = 535) and OLED (n = 103) are well represented.

What this means for you

If a large screen is non-negotiable, don't assume all "big TVs" cost the same to run. In this dataset, an 80"+ OLED averages roughly $100 less per year to run than an 80"+ standard LCD, and about $55 less than an 80"+ LCD (LED). The star rating and labelled kWh/year figure on the energy label — not the panel technology's reputation, and not screen size alone — is the number worth comparing in-store.

Bottom line

  • Screen size is the single biggest driver of a TV's running cost — budget for it, not just the purchase price.
  • Within any size band, running costs still vary — check the yellow energy label's kWh/year figure and star rating before you buy.
  • At very large sizes, OLED can be the cheaper option to run, not the more expensive one — don't rule it out on efficiency grounds alone.
