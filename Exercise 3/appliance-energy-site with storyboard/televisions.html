Televisions and energy use

If you're weighing up a screen upgrade — or you're the one stocking the shop floor — two questions usually matter most: which brands actually dominate what people buy, and how much more will a bigger screen cost to run? The data below comes from 4,724 television models registered with Australia's national Energy Rating scheme, and answers both.

Which brands actually dominate the market?

76 different TV brands are registered for sale in Australia — but buying decisions cluster around a handful of names. Share of the 4,724 registered models, by brand:

Samsung, Kogan and LG account for 57% of every registered model between them — the remaining 71 brands share the rest.

Do bigger screens really cost more to run?

Yes — and the jump is bigger than most people expect. Average yearly labelled energy consumption by screen-size band:

Small (<50")
64 kWh/yr
Medium (50–70")
84 kWh/yr
Large (>70")
462 kWh/yr
A large screen (70"+) uses roughly 7× the energy of a small one every year — screen size and energy use are strongly correlated (r = 0.86) across the registered models. Curious what that means in dollars for a specific model? Try the calculator below.

About this data: the brand and screen-size figures above come from the Australian Government's Energy Rating product registration database (a snapshot taken 15 February 2026, 4,724 models). It records what manufacturers register for sale, not actual household purchases or real-world viewing hours, so treat it as a picture of the market rather than a survey of homes.
